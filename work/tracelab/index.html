Build. Test. Decide. Remember.

A system for turning code, CAD, photos, tests and student reasoning into one source-linked engineering history.

The idea

Engineering teams create evidence constantly. The difficult part is remembering why something changed, what was tested, and which decision followed.

TraceLab connects that evidence into a history that can support notebooks, season handoff, contribution evidence, coach insight and future iteration.

Product thesisFrom activity to engineering memory

Work → why →
evidence → test →
decision → next.

The interface is designed around engineering causality rather than vanity metrics or a generic AI dashboard.

Capture asks for missing human context. Evidence preserves source provenance. Tests link to decisions. Handoff keeps failures, unresolved questions and reasoning alive for the next season.

Implemented areas include authentication and team roles, seasons and subsystems, append-only evidence, versioned annotations, iterations, tests, decisions, offline capture and evidence triage.

The wider system also includes Evidence Graph, Time Machine, Failure Library, search, handoff, contribution mapping, coach and organization views, notifications, policy controls and provenance-preserving exports.

Evidence Trace / Phase CSpatial workbench

A graph that
explains — not
decorates.

The old fixed SVG graph was replaced with a bounded, read-only spatial Trace. It renders stored relationships instead of inventing or editing them.

Desktop uses React Flow with custom evidence nodes, semantic relation edges, focus controls and an optional minimap. ELK adds layered placement after a deterministic source → iteration → test → decision fallback. Mobile intentionally uses focused evidence cards rather than a tiny infinite canvas.

TrustEvidence must remain evidence

Source first.
AI second.

Student authorship and source provenance stay distinct. AI is contextual and source-linked rather than becoming the center of the product.

The system includes policy-aware competition controls, an AI action log, privacy-oriented capture behavior, tenant isolation tests and provenance-preserving exports.
